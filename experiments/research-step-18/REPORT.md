## One comparison method

Before a new study, name the quantity the learned component can change. Bind
the exact artifact, strongest simple control, ordered inputs, evaluation
code, and full work budget. Separate fresh families or documents from the
data used for design. Count failed attempts and complete outputs throughout.

Report correct answers, confidence quality, retained capability, and cost as
separate measurements. Use the study's independent unit for uncertainty:
repeated passes over one family share that family. Preserve the declared
source and task slices so an aggregate gain leaves individual losses visible.

Choose the next experiment by the decision it can change, its ability to
distinguish explanations, its readiness, and its expected cost. Forecaster
disagreement can help rank ready experiments. Shared uncertainty and a decisive
control also justify a test. The Solomon source audit is a concrete example:
it settled answer ownership and changed the next experiment before a cloud run.
