- Status: recovered and settled

- Evidence authority: `corpus_proxy`

- Device: CPU on arm64 macOS; MPS was unavailable in this lab execution context

## Question

Can the public `staccs/holo-llm-hrr-attention` checkout execute the published
T=512 three-arm comparison with the declared seed, architecture, optimizer,
and training budget using a public corpus?

This is an independent implementation/protocol verification. It is not an
exact reproduction of the upstream Telegram/MPS numbers because the public
checkout does not contain the upstream private Telegram corpus.

## Results

| Arm | Parameters | Final validation bits/byte | Median ms/step | Tokens/sec |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Softmax | 4.330M | **2.9731** | **192.99** | **42,448** |
| Uniform HRR | 4.068M | 3.5507 | 226.95 | 36,096 |
| Gated HRR | 4.069M | 3.3308 | 296.08 | 27,668 |

At this T=512 public-corpus run, both HRR variants were slower and had worse
validation bits/byte than softmax. The directional ordering matches the
upstream T=512 report, but the absolute values must not be compared across
corpora or devices.

## Conclusion

The public Holo HRR implementation is independently executable under the
declared T=512 protocol on a public corpus. This result supports the claim
that HRR attention is a functioning alternative implementation, but it does
not support a T=512 quality or speed advantage over softmax. Exact replication
of the upstream Telegram/MPS measurements remains open until that corpus and
matching hardware conditions are published.
